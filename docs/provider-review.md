# felt air: words for a provider to review

Oct 17, 2026. Every word in felt air that touches care, in one place, for conversations with providers. The app is not
medical advice and says so; this sheet is how we find out what a provider would actually say, and what they'd never want
an app to say. Copy is quoted exactly as it appears (sources in `ios/AsthmaCore`). Mark each line: keep, change (to what),
or remove.

## 1. What we call things

| On screen | Means | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| **Rescue** | A use of the rescue (reliever) inhaler | Do patients say "rescue", "reliever", or the inhaler's name? |
| **Standard** | A dose of the daily (controller / preventer) inhaler | Is "Standard" clear, or should it be "Daily" / "Preventer"? |
| **Flare day** | A day with rescue use | Is "flare" the right word? Too dramatic, too vague? |
| **Baseline** | An ordinary hour with no inhaler use, which felt air compares against | Does this need explaining to patients? |
| **Note** | Something the person said with no inhaler logged | Would "symptom" or "felt off" be more useful? |

## 2. How a day compares (the verdict)

- "Matches your flare days"
- "A bit like your flare days"
- "Looks like your baseline days"
- "Still learning" (before there's enough to compare)

Ask: does "matches your flare days" read as a warning or a prediction? We never give a percent or say an attack will happen.

## 3. What felt air says about a moment

- "High weed pollen often shows up on your flare days."
- "High weed pollen was there. It often shows up on your flare days." (a past rescue)
- "Thursday afternoon looks more like your flare days: high ozone."
- "Couldn't read the air here just now. Logging still works."

Facts only: never "this caused", "this triggered", or "likely". Ask: is "often shows up on your flare days" understood as
association, not cause?

## 4. Profiles (who you are with the air, so far)

| Name | Line |
|---|---|
| Weather-sensitive | Your flare days follow the weather: heat, cold snaps or humid air. |
| Allergen-led | Your flare days line up with living things in the air: pollen, pets or mold. |
| Air-quality-led | Your flare days line up with what's floating in the air: ozone, fine particles or smoke. |
| Effort-led | Your flare days come with effort: exercise, or laughing or crying hard. |
| Hot, hazy days | Your flare days tend to be hot, with ozone or fine particles up. |
| Seasonal | Your flare days follow the seasons: the weather and what's growing. |
| Airborne | Your flare days line up with what's in the air, living or not: pollen, particles, smoke. |
| Mixed | Several kinds of things show up on your flare days: … |
| Nothing stands out yet | Nothing in the air shows up much more on your flare days than on others. That's worth knowing too. |

Shown as "Learned · may shift with the seasons". Ask: do these map to how you think about asthma phenotypes or triggers?
Would any of them mislead a patient (e.g. "Effort-led" read as "exercise-induced asthma")?

Strength words: **Often · Sometimes · Maybe · No more than usual · Not tracked yet**. No numbers on screen.

## 5. Advice (a provocation, not guidance)

Shown on Now and for the forecast, split by where you are: **staying put** (at home) or **going out**. Every card has
"A suggestion, not medical advice. If a flare is severe, get medical help now." behind an "i".

| When | Staying put | Going out |
|---|---|---|
| Wildfire smoke | Close your windows and set your AC to recirculate to keep smoke out. | If you need to go outside, an N95 mask filters out fine particles. |
| Storm while pollen is high | Keep your windows shut until the storm has passed. | If you've been outside, shower and change clothes when you get in, to rinse off pollen. |
| Sharp temperature drop | Keep the air indoors humidified if you can. | Bring your inhaler. Breathe through your nose, or wear a loose scarf to warm the air. |
| Rainy, humid and warm (mold) | A dehumidifier can keep indoor humidity below 50%. | Watch for damp, shaded spots, where mold spores gather. |
| Fine particles high | Run your indoor air purifier if you have one. | Try to avoid walking along busy main roads. |
| Dusty day (PM10) | A damp cloth clears settled dust from surfaces. | A standard mask can help filter out coarse dust. |
| Traffic pollution (NO₂) | Keep your windows closed during rush hour. | Consider a route through a park or side streets. |
| Ozone high | Stay indoors during the hottest part of the day. | Try to move outdoor exercise to the morning. |
| Tree pollen high | Keep your windows closed to keep it out. | Sunglasses help keep pollen out of your eyes. |
| Grass pollen high | If you have a lawn, try not to mow it today. | Shower and change clothes when you get home. |
| Weed pollen high | Run your indoor air filter if you have one. | Try to avoid walking through overgrown fields or lots. |

After a flare, felt air only states what was in the air: "Wildfire smoke was in the air before this.", "It was 16° colder
than three hours before." Ask, per line: would you say this to a patient? What's missing (e.g. "check your action plan")?
Is "Bring your inhaler" appropriate for an app to say?

## 6. Your body (from the phone and Apple Health, in words)

- Activity: "Mostly still" · "Some walking" · "A fair bit of walking" · "A lot of walking"
- Sleep: "A short night" · "A usual night" · "A long night"
- Heart and breathing rate, against the person's own month: "About your usual" · "A bit higher than usual" ·
  "Higher than usual" · "Lower than usual"

Ask: is comparing heart and breathing rate to "your usual" helpful or alarming? What would you want to see next to a flare?

## 7. Prompts

- After a rescue: "What's going on?" / "Say what was going on"
- Lock Screen, standby: "Need your inhaler? Tap the mic to log it and talk."
- Morning: "Today: a bit like your flare days" + the look-ahead line.
- Evening: "Add what was going on, so felt air can learn what your flare days share."

Ask: is a prompt mid-flare welcome or intrusive? Is "Need your inhaler?" on the Lock Screen OK?

## 8. Always

- "Outdoor air only. Not medical advice, and not a prediction."
- "A suggestion, not medical advice. If a flare is severe, get medical help now."

Ask: is this enough, and is "get medical help now" the right instruction?
