# What the guidelines and the research say, and how felt air uses it

Oct 18, 2026. Written as slides: each `##` is one slide, with the line to say and its sources. Drop into a
deck as-is. Sources marked ✓ were checked this week (title, authors, journal, year); the rest are well known
but weren't re-checked, so confirm the link before the talk. felt air is **not** a medical device and
gives no medical advice; everything below is how we word facts and when we point people to care.

---

## 1. The idea in one line

**felt air writes down the air around every rescue puff, and shows what your flare days have in common.**
It never says what *caused* them, never gives a risk percentage, never says "attack". It compares your
flare days with your ordinary (baseline) days, and only speaks once it has enough of both.

Notes: the gate is 8 flare moments, 20 baseline moments, 4 in the level, and lift above 1.25 (AGENTS.md).

## 2. When we say "check in": rescue use

Two rules, in the guidelines' own terms, shown as a card on Now with the source named:

| What happened | What felt air says | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| Rescue inhaler again **within 4 hours** | "Many asthma action plans say needing it more than every 4 hours is a sign to get help now. Follow your action plan. If it isn't helping or breathing is hard, call 911." | US asthma action plans built on the NHLBI template list needing quick-relief medicine more than every 4 hours as a red-zone sign (e.g. Minnesota Dept. of Health adult and youth plans). |
| Rescue on **more than 2 days in a week** | "Guidelines say using it on more than 2 days a week can mean asthma isn't well controlled. It's worth checking in with your doctor about your plan." | NAEPP EPR-3 (2007) control criteria; the 2020 focused update: SABA use >2 days/week for symptom relief "generally indicates inadequate control" ✓. GINA counts reliever use more than twice a week as one of four control questions. |

Sources:
- ✓ Expert Panel Working Group of the NHLBI. *2020 Focused Updates to the Asthma Management Guidelines.*
  J Allergy Clin Immunol. 2020;146(6):1217–1270. doi:10.1016/j.jaci.2020.10.003 · [PMC7924476](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7924476)
- NAEPP. *Expert Panel Report 3: Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Management of Asthma.* NHLBI, 2007.
- Global Initiative for Asthma. *Global Strategy for Asthma Management and Prevention*, 2024 update. ginasthma.org
- Minnesota Department of Health. *Asthma Action Plan* (adult and youth). [planadult.pdf](https://www.health.mn.gov/diseases/asthma/professionals/planadult.pdf), [planyouth.pdf](https://www.health.mn.gov/diseases/asthma/professionals/planyouth.pdf)

Notes: pre-exercise puffs don't count toward "inadequate control" in the guidelines; we can't tell them
apart yet, so the card says "can mean", and "Got it" puts it away for three days.

## 3. Test sprays aren't puffs (priming)

Metered-dose inhalers are primed by spraying into the air before first use and after they've sat unused:
Ventolin HFA 4 sprays, ProAir HFA 3, quickly. Real puffs are usually 30–60 seconds apart. So 3+ presses
inside 10 seconds get a question, not a log: **"Were those test sprays? Like priming a new inhaler."**

Sources: the products' patient information leaflets (Ventolin HFA, ProAir HFA). Confirm the current
leaflets before quoting counts.

## 4. Archetype: allergic, pollen and mold (Allergens)

**What it is:** allergic asthma is the best-established phenotype; pollen seasons and mold spores line up
with emergency visits. **Thunderstorm asthma** is the dramatic case: rain ruptures pollen into breathable
fragments.

**Signals felt air uses:** tree, grass and weed pollen (Ambee counts and NAB-style risk); mold weather
(rain, humid and warm); journal tags: pets, mold, dust.

**What we can say:** "High weed pollen showed up often on your flare days." Never "you're allergic to".

Sources:
- ✓ Erbas B, et al. *Outdoor pollen is a trigger of child and adolescent asthma emergency department
  presentations: a systematic review and meta-analysis.* Allergy. 2018;73(8):1632–1641. doi:10.1111/all.13407
  (grass pollen: +1.88% ED presentations per 10 grains/m³).
- ✓ Thien F, et al. *The Melbourne epidemic thunderstorm asthma event 2016.* Lancet Planet Health.
  2018;2(6):e255–e263. doi:10.1016/S2542-5196(18)30120-7
- Targonski PV, Persky VW, Ramekrishnan V. *Effect of environmental molds on risk of death from asthma
  during the pollen season.* J Allergy Clin Immunol. 1995;95(5):955–961.
- Wenzel SE. *Asthma phenotypes: the evolution from clinical to molecular approaches.* Nat Med. 2012;18:716–725.

## 5. Archetype: pollution-sensitive (Pollutants)

**What it is:** fine particles (PM2.5), ozone and NO₂ are linked to more asthma ED visits and admissions.
Smoke from wildfires is the extreme case.

**Signals felt air uses:** PM2.5, PM10, ozone, NO₂ (OpenAQ nearest monitor, AirNow, Ambee); the EPA's
"Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups" level as "high"; wildfire smoke.

**What we can say:** "Ozone was high." underlined if it's yours; the exposure question: "Fine particles were
high around then. Were you outside?"

Sources:
- ✓ Orellano P, Quaranta N, Reynoso J, Balbi B, Vasquez J. *Effect of outdoor air pollution on asthma
  exacerbations in children and adults: systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis.* PLoS One.
  2017;12(3):e0174050. [PMC5358780](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5358780) (PM2.5 OR 1.028; NO₂ OR 1.024).
- Guarnieri M, Balmes JR. *Outdoor air pollution and asthma.* Lancet. 2014;383(9928):1581–1592.
- US EPA. *AQI Basics*: at 101+ ("Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups"), people with asthma should reduce
  prolonged or heavy outdoor exertion. airnow.gov

## 6. Archetype: weather-sensitive (Weather dynamics)

**What it is:** cold, dry air narrows airways, especially when breathing hard; sudden temperature drops,
heat and humidity show up in studies of asthma visits.

**Signals felt air uses:** temperature bands, humidity, a sharp drop (10°F+ in 3 hours), heat alerts;
the exposure question for cold: "It was cold out. Were you breathing cold air?"

Sources:
- ✓ Koskela HO. *Cold air-provoked respiratory symptoms: the mechanisms and management.* Int J
  Circumpolar Health. 2007;66(2):91–100. PMID 17515249.
- NAEPP EPR-3 (2007): weather and cold air among the factors to ask patients about.

Notes: temperature-change and humidity studies vary by place; we present weather as facts on your days,
not a mechanism.

## 7. Archetype: effort-linked (Self)

**What it is:** exercise-induced bronchoconstriction is common in asthma (and occurs without it); cold or
polluted air while exercising makes it more likely.

**Signals felt air uses:** new this week, **passively**: a Health workout in the hour before, running or
cycling (Motion), or a burst of steps. Plus what you say: exercise, stress, laughing or crying hard.

**What we can say:** "You'd been active." underlined if it's yours. Combined archetypes: "Outdoor effort"
(pollutants + effort), "Weather and effort".

Sources:
- ✓ Parsons JP, Hallstrand TS, et al. *An Official American Thoracic Society Clinical Practice Guideline:
  Exercise-induced Bronchoconstriction.* Am J Respir Crit Care Med. 2013;187(9):1016–1027.
  doi:10.1164/rccm.201303-0437ST

## 8. What we can't see (and say so)

- **Viral infections** (the most common exacerbation trigger in the literature): only if you say "cold or
  flu" in a note; it then counts under Self, never as air.
- **Indoor air** (gas stoves, cleaning products, scents, dust mites, pets at home): journal tags only.
  Every reading is labelled **outdoor**.
- **Controller adherence, inhaler technique**: not tracked. The care card points to your plan and doctor.

---

## 9. How a pattern gets said (the honesty rules)

1. Code finds the pattern (counts, lift, gate); the language model never computes it.
2. Compare flare days with baseline days; unsampled hours are missing, not "no".
3. Two facts at most per sentence; each is underlined only if it's one of yours.
4. Facts, never causes. No percentages. Never "attack". A forecast "looks", never "will".
5. Demo and sample data (Ambee sample, demo scenarios) are shown, never in patterns.
