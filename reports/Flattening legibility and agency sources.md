# Openable summaries hand agency back

The research supports a narrower thesis than "indexing flattens and takes agency away", and the narrow one is the one that survives a design-academic audience. Abstraction is necessary and often helps people; it removes agency when the summary cannot be walked back to what it was made from and the person cannot act on what they find (refinement a), or when a figure about a population is presented as a statement about this person (refinement b). Each clause has a source: Simon and Gigerenzer for the concession, Latour and the OECD's own composite-indicator handbook for (a), Molenaar, Sen and Bowker and Star for (b), and Ananny and Crawford for the warning that seeing is not the same as being able to act. For felt air, the best evidence is the EPA's own paperwork: its worked example reports an AQI of 126 for a day on which two pollutants are over 100 and a third is at 90, and its 1999 rule says a breakpoint "does not imply any sort of health-effects threshold". Three cautions matter before next week. First, most of the theory canon was confirmed only at record or abstract level and the best-known quotations (Scott, Porter, Latour, Bowker and Star) come from memory or secondary pages, so they must be checked in the books. Second, no source was found for the app's line "For most people, cold starts at freezing", which is the picture the ledger says to lead with. Third, nothing has been tested with people, so "agency" is argued, not observed. Four absences in the literature look like the author's own ground: no study shows a personal threshold beside a population one, no scholarly critique of bird-identification apps was found, no precedent combines pure colour with infinite scroll, and no source reconciles the user-side and state-side senses of "legibility".

## How to read the marks in this report

The six research notes used five different marking schemes. This report folds them into three, and every source below carries one. **[primary]** means the researcher read the primary or publisher page, or the full text, during this research. **[record]** means the citation was confirmed but the content was seen only as an abstract, a catalogue or repository record, a review, a press release or a search summary; **[record*]** marks the weaker cases where even the abstract was seen only in fragments. **[memory]** means the item was not checked at all and comes from the researcher's recall; treat it as a lead. Two limits apply across the board. No paper in the HCI set was read in full, and pages in the AI set were read through a summarising tool, so every quotation in those two areas needs one check against the published text, including those marked [primary]. No page numbers are given anywhere, because none were verified. A table of the specific quotes and facts to check before use is near the end.

## Flattening is commensuration with the path back removed

### The word has no settled meaning, so the thesis must supply one

"Flattening" is not a technical term in science and technology studies. The notes found three uses that differ from each other: homogenised culture in Chayka's *Filterworld* [record], which reviewers say is asserted more than shown ([Yale Review](https://yalereview.org/article/anna-shechtman-algorithm-kyle-chayka-taylor-lorenz)); the "mathematical, flattened, infinite, continuous, and homogenous space" of linear perspective in Steyerl's "In Free Fall" [primary] ([e-flux](https://www.e-flux.com/journal/24/67860/in-free-fall-a-thought-experiment-on-vertical-perspective/)); and the flat, mobile inscription that lets someone master the world at a distance in Latour's "Visualisation and Cognition" [record; the list of properties is from memory] ([Latour's site](https://www.bruno-latour.fr/node/293.html)). The author's own published writing does not use "flatten", "index" in this sense, "black box" or "cluster" at all; its vocabulary is legible, opaque, hidden, invisible and auditable ([The Legible Web](https://pandji.co/writing/the-legible-web)) [primary]. So the framing is new and unpublished, and the audience will ask for a definition.

The notes propose one built from established terms: **flattening is commensuration that has been black-boxed, so that reference no longer circulates back.** Commensuration is Espeland and Stevens's name for comparing different things by a common metric, which reduces information and turns qualities into quantities ([Annual Reviews](https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/10.1146/annurev.soc.24.1.313)) [record]. Black-boxing is Latour's name for technical work "made invisible by its own success" [quote confirmed only through secondary pages] ([Wikipedia, citing Latour 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackboxing)). "Circulating reference", from chapter 2 of *Pandora's Hope*, is the idea that an abstraction is legitimate while the chain of transformations stays traceable in both directions [memory; a search did not retrieve it]. This is the best theoretical ground for refinement (a) and it is the least verified, so that chapter is the first thing to read. The notes also separate two operations that the thesis should connect in a sentence: hiding places a mechanism out of view, flattening reduces many values to one, and an index hides by summarising.

### Refinement (a) is a professional norm, not an outsider's complaint

The strongest move for (a) is to cite the people who build indexes. The OECD and European Commission *Handbook on Constructing Composite Indicators* defends composites as tools for summarising and communicating, on the condition that they can be decomposed back into their parts and tested for sensitivity ([JRC full text](https://knowledge4policy.ec.europa.eu/sites/default/files/jrc47008_handbook_final.pdf)) [record; the step names, including "back to the detail", are from memory and the pros-and-cons table was not retrieved]. Paired with Latour, this gives (a) a two-sided defence: theory says reference must circulate back, and statistical practice says composites must decompose. Davis, Kingsbury and Merry add that an indicator carries an embedded theory of what is good and shifts power to whoever produces it ([IILJ](https://www.iilj.org/publications/indicators-technology-global-governance/)) [record]. Scott himself fits (a) better than he fits the broad thesis. His claim is narrower than it is usually quoted: simplification is normal statecraft, and disaster needs legibility plus high-modernist ideology, an authoritarian state and a weak civil society together; the harm sits in imposition without recourse ([Sunstein's review](https://home.uchicago.edu/csunstei/moreisless.html)) [record for the book; Sunstein's text primary]. Three sources complicate (a) and should be named before a critic names them. Drucker's "data are capta, taken not given" [primary] ([DHQ](https://dhq.digitalhumanities.org/vol/5/1/000091/000091.html)) and Gitelman's *"Raw Data" Is an Oxymoron* [record] mean a summary opens into another construction, not into raw truth; the answer is that (a) asks for a route to a less processed layer and its conditions of capture. Ananny and Crawford ask, "can 'black boxes' ever be opened, and if so, would that ever be sufficient?" [record; abstract only, and their ten limits could not be listed] ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444816676645)); the answer is that "opens" must mean the person can do something with what they find. Star and Strauss show that making things visible is double-edged, bringing surveillance as well as recognition ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1023/A:1008651105359.pdf)) [record].

### Refinement (b) has a formal argument behind it

Molenaar's 2004 manifesto is the most direct scientific support for (b): findings about variation between people apply to one person's variation over time only under ergodicity, which human processes rarely satisfy ([citing article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12124-007-9011-1)) [record]. Sen's capability approach supplies two usable terms, the "informational basis" of an evaluation (what it lets in and what it excludes) and conversion factors (why the same conditions become different outcomes for different people) ([Stanford Encyclopedia](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/)) [record for the concept; *Development as Freedom* itself is memory]. Bowker and Star's "torque" names the twisting of a life read through a category built for a population ([MIT Press Bookstore](https://mitpressbookstore.mit.edu/book/9780262522953)) [record; exact wording not read in the book]. Hacking gives the history of how the population's number became the standard for the individual ("Biopower and the Avalanche of Printed Numbers") [record; page range unverified] and the mechanism by which classified people respond, "The target has moved. I call this the 'looping effect'" ([LRB](https://lrb.co.uk/v28/n16/ian-hacking/making-up-people)) [primary]. Nguyen's "Value Capture" is the strongest recent philosophical support for the agency claim: a person's rich values are replaced by a simplified metric supplied from outside ([JESP](https://jesp.org/index.php/jesp/article/view/3048/494)) [record]. The complication for (b) is Fourcade and Healy's "Seeing Like a Market": platform scores are highly individual and still disempowering, so "your own number" is not automatically safe ([author page](https://kieranhealy.org/publications/slam/)) [record]. The answer is that (b) and (a) work together: a personal number that cannot be opened, and that someone else owns, fails (a).

### "Legibility" names who reads whom

The author defines legibility from the user's side: "whether a user can see and understand what a web technology is doing, who controls it and what it does with their behavior" ([The Legible Web](https://pandji.co/writing/the-legible-web)) [primary]. Scott's legibility runs the other way: the state makes people readable in order to tax, conscript and administer. The author does not cite Scott, and the two senses pull in opposite directions. The notes offer a reconciliation, and no source was found that already makes it: **legibility is directional, and the same word names who gets to read whom.** The early web was legible to the person (view source) and illegible to the platform; the platform web is legible to the platform (users as data) and illegible to the person (compiled code, ranked feeds). Flattening is then the operation that buys legibility for the governing reader at the expense of the governed one, and the author's four works each try to turn the reading direction around. Rao's 2010 essay is the standard bridge from Scott to technology culture and gives a quotable form of the failure: it begins when an observer attributes their own incomprehension "to the irrationality of what you are looking at" ([Ribbonfarm](https://www.ribbonfarm.com/2010/07/26/a-big-little-idea-called-legibility/)) [primary]. The AQI fits this reading. The reasons agencies prefer one number (a legal duty to report to a whole metropolitan area, comparability between cities, one trigger for alerts, a tie to the standard they enforce) are all needs of the sender; this list is the researcher's assembly from EPA's documents, not a quoted finding. Two critics of Scott keep the reconciliation honest: Li says to stop asking whether a scheme fails and ask what it does and for whom, and that "the system" is rarely one actor ([author page](https://www.taniali.org/papers/beyond-the-state-and-failed-schemes)) [record]; Ferguson says power can work through selective legibility, leaving most territory unread [record for existence; pages and DOI from memory].

### "Agency" needs pinning to one sense

Bennett and colleagues reviewed 161 HCI papers and found that understandings of agency and autonomy "remain ambiguous" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.12490)) [record]. The author's published use ties agency to understanding and consent ("A user who cannot read what a technology is doing cannot meaningfully consent to it"), not to control or customisation. The sources converge on two tests that match the refinements: can the person inspect and contest the abstraction (Illich's convivial tools [record; quote secondary], Kluttz, Kohli and Mulligan on contestability having "distinct advantages over transparency and explainability" ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3311894)) [record]), and is it about them or about a population. Williams's *Stand Out of Our Light* is the safest agency citation for the attention work because it is open access and can be quoted directly ([Cambridge Core](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/stand-out-of-our-light/3F8D7BA2C0FE3A7126A4D9B73A89415D)) [primary]. Christin's review of Mau warns that critiques of metrics tend to assert effects on people in the future tense ([review](https://cambridge.org/core/journals/european-journal-of-sociology-archives-europeennes-de-sociologie/article/abs/powerful-metrics-steffen-mau-the-metric-society-on-the-quantification-of-the-social-cambridge-uk-polity-2019-200-p/DD52661A32E870E16AA9F6DF725DBB19)) [record]. The safe form of the claim is about what an interface allows (no way to trace, question or see alternatives), not about a measured effect on people.

## The EPA's own documents make the case for felt air

### What the colour is made from

The AQI is a chain of deliberate reductions, and each one is documented by its maker. EPA's technical assistance document tells agencies to "identify the highest concentration among all of the monitors within each reporting area", interpolate each pollutant onto a 0 to 500 scale, and report only the highest: "The AQI is the highest value calculated for each pollutant" ([EPA TAD, May 2026](https://document.airnow.gov/technical-assistance-document-for-the-reporting-of-daily-air-quailty.pdf)) [primary]. So the index contains **two maximum operators**, the worst monitor and then the worst pollutant. EPA's worked example shows the effect: ozone at 126, PM2.5 at 102 and carbon monoxide at 90 are reported as "The AQI is 126, with O3 as the main pollutant". The same document says the official figure is in effect yesterday's, that particle levels "can vary considerably, even over small distances", and that pollutant concentrations and other pollutants' values are voluntary to report. The 1999 rule says 100 is pinned to the legal standard, that the PM2.5 breakpoint "does not imply any sort of health-effects threshold", and that orange was chosen because red sends "too strong a message" ([64 FR 42530](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-08-04/html/99-19433.htm)) [primary]. The 2024 update lowered the Good/Moderate breakpoint from 12.0 to 9.0 µg/m³, and EPA's own answer to "Did EPA update the AQI because my air quality is getting worse?" is "No" ([EPA fact sheet](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2024-02/pm-naaqs-air-quality-index-fact-sheet.pdf)) [primary]. The same air changed colour. That one fact shows the colour is a policy setting, and it serves both sides: the index is maintained against new science, and it is not a property of the air.

Index designers named the defect long ago. Swamee and Tyagi's abstract says eclipsed aggregations give false reassurance and that "the maximum operator does not account for changes in the remaining pollutants" ([journal index record](https://acnpsearch.tweb-dev.unibo.it/singlejournalindex/47484895)) [record]; Ott's 1978 *Environmental Indices* is the canonical source but was seen only as a catalogue record, so his definitions of "eclipsing" and "ambiguity" are unverified. Health researchers found the consequence: across 1,373,165 adult respiratory emergency visits in California, the AQI tracked visits in the cool season (pooled RR 1.03) and showed no significant association in Southern California's warm season, when the mixture is complex ([Cromar et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7671501/)) [primary]. On the person's side, a review of 18 studies put the median within-person correlation between ambient and personal PM2.5 at 0.54, ranging from 0.09 to 0.83 ([Avery et al. 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/20075021)) [primary], and "79 percent of US counties do not have a PM2.5 monitor" ([RFF press release](https://www.rff.org/news/press-releases/new-satellite-data-show-twice-as-many-americans-live-in-counties-not-meeting-fine-particulate-air-quality-standards-than-previously-thought/)) [record; press release, not the paper].

The closest precedent is also the foil. The Louisville inhaler-sensor study joined 5,660 timestamped rescue-inhaler events from 140 people to conditions, but its unit of analysis was the day, only 23% of events had a location, pollen came from one rooftop monitor, and the authors "did not have" data on individual sensitivity ([Su et al. 2017](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5289907/)) [primary]. It proves the mechanism and answers a population question for city policy. felt air returns the pattern to the person who made it. The Louisville limits read like a specification for felt air's provenance line.

And asthma apps are not known to improve outcomes: a review of 12 controlled studies found inconsistent effects and noted that "No studies explicitly reported adoption of and adherence to the technology system" ([Hui et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7651908)) [record]. felt air is a design argument, not a clinical one, and should be presented that way.

### Precedents for felt air

Every air precedent in the notes chooses what to hide, which is the honest frame: felt air chooses differently and shows its choices; it does not escape reduction. Pinsky's *Pollution Pods* turns an index into a room and flattens each city to one recipe ([artist's page](https://www.michaelpinsky.com/?p=3844)) [primary]. Polli's *Particle Falls* shows this street in these fifteen seconds and hides composition ([Science History Institute](https://www.sciencehistory.org/particle-falls)) [record]. Calvillo's *Yellow Dust* hides the number on purpose and is the most direct artistic critique of the AQI colour scale found [primary]; her earlier *In the Air* shows each pollutant separately but interpolates air between stations ([Design Observer](https://designobserver.com/feature/in-the-air-of-madrid/23028)) [record], which is the false reveal that felt air's halftone dots refuse. Citizen Sense's *Dustbox* and its "Data Stories" join readings to residents' accounts ([Citizen Sense](https://citizensense.net/kits/dustbox-hardware/)) [primary], and HabitatMap's AirCasting records one person's path through a city ([HabitatMap](https://www.habitatmap.org/about)) [primary]; these two are closest. None compares one person's bad days with the same person's ordinary days, and installations work at the scale of a visit while a diary works over months. From the HCI side the lineage is Jeremijenko's Dangling String ([Weiser and Brown](https://calmtech.com/papers/designing-calm-technology)) [primary], UbiFit Garden [record*], Health Mashups [record*] and *Dear Data* ([MoMA](https://www.moma.org/collection/works/216739)) [record], with Oura's Readiness Score and WHOOP Recovery as the counter-model of one opaque, colour-banded daily number. Apple's Cycle Tracking, which the repo names as inspiration for drawing predicted days differently, was not verified [memory].

## The other three works each have a core literature and a foil

### Birdsong: the flattening is literal, and nobody has written the critique

This is the best-evidenced ground in the whole thesis, because the people who build the tools say it themselves. Wattenberg, Viégas and Johnson's section headings are the argument: "Cluster sizes in a t-SNE plot mean nothing", "Distances between clusters might not mean anything", "Random noise doesn't always look random" ([Distill](https://distill.pub/2016/misread-tsne/)) [primary]. Chari and Pachter write that "extreme dimension reduction, from hundreds or thousands of dimensions to 2, inevitably induces significant distortion", and fit the same data to the shape of an elephant with comparable scores ([PLOS Computational Biology](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011288)) [primary; a correction was published in November 2025 and should be read; the domain is genomics]. The step is one-way: a point's features cannot be recovered from its position on the map. That is refinement (a) in its exact technical sense. Burrell's three opacities (secrecy, illiteracy, and the mismatch between high-dimensional computation and human-scale reading) let the work say which kind it addresses and avoid the reply "the code is open source" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951715622512)) [record]. Crawford and Paglen supply the verb: ImageNet's labels "compress and simplify images into deadpan banalities" ([Excavating AI](https://excavating.ai/)) [primary]. Pasquinelli and Joler treat machine learning as an instrument of "information compression" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7680082)) [record], and Steyerl's "Mean Images" gives the plainest line: "They replace likenesses with likelinesses" ([New Left Review](https://newleftreview.org/issues/ii140/articles/hito-steyerl-mean-images)) [primary].

For birds specifically, Cornell's own account says "Merlin converts the audio into an image called a spectrogram" and hands it to a vision model trained on 140 hours of expert-marked bird sound and 126 hours of non-bird background ([Macaulay Library](https://www.macaulaylibrary.org/2021/06/22/behind-the-scenes-of-sound-id-in-merlin/)) [primary]. A field study found that "Merlin disagreed with itself on 57% of point counts when running on 2 different devices at the same time" ([Ornithological Applications](https://academic.oup.com/condor/article/127/4/1/8222742)) [primary]. BirdNET covers a fixed list of 984 species and drops from 0.791 on clean clips to 0.414 on real soundscapes ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecoinf.2021.101236)) [record; figures from a mirror of the abstract]. The chain from living bird to recording to three-second window to spectrogram to vector to species name, and the list of what each step drops (the individual, dialect, behaviour, anything outside the list), is the researcher's inference; no source sets it out. The sources that complicate the case are strong. Ghani and colleagues show that the embedding keeps enough structure to be reused for other animals with a few examples ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06292)) [primary, abstract]: the interface throws the rest away, not the vector. Seaver argues that auditing "enacts an understanding of algorithms as black boxes" ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/2053951717738104)) [record], so a piece that stages a sealed box reinforces the myth. Kaur and colleagues found that data scientists "over-trust and misuse interpretability tools" ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/interpreting-interpretability-understanding-data-scientists-use-of-interpretability-tools-for-machine-learning/)) [record], and Bansal and colleagues that explanations "increased the chance that humans will accept the AI's recommendation, regardless of its correctness" ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.14779)) [primary, abstract]. Even Distill's Activation Atlas is built by projecting to two dimensions and averaging [primary]. The constructive vocabulary is contestability and seams: Ehsan and colleagues' "Seamful XAI" ties revealing seams to user agency in so many words ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.06753)) [primary, abstract].

The precedents sort by whether the viewer can get from the summary back to its sources. Those that pass most fully show the data and the labour: Ridler's *Myriad (Tulips)*, with a handwritten label under each photograph ([artist's page](https://annaridler.com/myriad-tulips)) [primary]; Schmitt's *Declassifier*, which overlays the training images a label was learned from and is the clearest existing example of opening a summary back up ([Juxtapoz](https://www.juxtapoz.com/news/photography/humans-of-ai-philipp-schmitt-s-algorithm-generated-collages/)) [record; press only, date from memory]; *Exposing.ai* ([about page](https://exposing.ai/about/)) [record]; and *Models All The Way Down* ([Knowing Machines](https://knowingmachines.org/models-all-the-way)) [primary; year not on the page]. *ImageNet Roulette* turned the classifier on the viewer, and ImageNet then announced removal of 438 people categories and 600,040 images ([The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2019/09/23/leading-online-database-to-remove-600000-images-after-art-project-reveals-its-racist-bias)) [record]: evidence that exposure can change the system exposed. *Gender Shades* reopened one accuracy figure into subgroups, from 0.8% error to "up to 34.7%" ([PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v81/buolamwini18a.html)) [primary]. The two works to define the piece against are Google Creative Lab's *Bird Sounds*, whose page says "similar sounds are placed closer together", the exact reading Wattenberg and colleagues say fails between clusters ([project page](https://experiments.withgoogle.com/bird-sounds)) [primary; the site lists May 2017, not 2016], and Anadol's *Unsupervised*, of which Lossin writes "The AI is not... 'seeing' images" ([e-flux Criticism](https://www.e-flux.com/criticism/527236/refik-anadol-s-unsupervised)) [primary]. One fact matters to the argument and is unconfirmed: whether *Bird Sounds* lets a viewer play the source recording for each point, which would mean it opens to the item though not to the reasons. One more caution: no birdsong project appears on the author's site, GitHub or Are.na, so the audience has nothing published to refer to.

### Color scroller: argue designed loss of agency, not addiction

The scroller's README says it lays "the mechanics of digital addiction... bare" with a fake leaderboard, churn prediction and a debug panel ([README](https://github.com/apandji/ColorScroller)) [primary]. The HCI literature now names what it shows. Monge Roffarello, Lukoff and De Russis reviewed 43 papers and list infinite scroll among eleven "attention capture damaging patterns" ([Politecnico di Torino](https://iris.polito.it/handle/11583/2978380)) [record]. Lukoff and colleagues found that autoplay and recommendations most undermined sense of agency among 120 YouTube users ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11778)) [record]. Holte and Ferraro found that grayscale cut daily screen time by about 38 minutes among 161 undergraduates ([abstract record](https://www.x-mol.com/paper/1349793630393233408)) [record], which supports colour as a working part of the mechanism, though later grayscale work is mixed. Grosser's "What Do Metrics Want?" is the closest precedent for an artwork-plus-essay pair: "The primary thing that Facebook metrics want is increased user engagement with the site" ([Computational Culture](https://computationalculture.net/what-do-metrics-want/)) [primary]. The word "addiction" is the weak point. Panova and Carbonell find insufficient support for "smartphone addiction" and propose "problematic use" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6174603)) [primary]; Orben and Przybylski find technology use explains at most 0.4% of variation in adolescent well-being ([Oxford ORA](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:5d844350-a359-47d3-b10c-4bfb93ce613b)) [record]; and Baughan and colleagues argue from inside HCI that normative dissociation, absorption with the stopping point taken away, is the better frame ([EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/953637)) [record]. The defensible claim is that the work exposes hidden design that reduces a measured sense of agency. Schüll's *Addiction by Design* is the obvious source for the gambling lineage the author already asserts, and it is entirely unverified here [memory], as are Eyal, Fogg, Zuboff, Wu and Odell.

Grosser's method is subtraction: remove one layer so the remaining one shows. *The Endless Doomscroller* strips headlines to generic doom and is the nearest verified precedent ([project page](https://bengrosser.com/projects/endless-doomscroller/)) [primary]; *Facebook Demetricator*, *Safebook* and *Minus* ("only 100 posts—for life") belong beside it [primary]. Moniker's *Clickclickclick.click* narrates the visitor's own tracking back to them ([Moniker](https://studiomoniker.com/projects/click-click-click)) [primary], and Depoorter's *Die With Me* builds in a hard stopping cue ([artist's page](https://driesdepoorter.be/diewithme/)) [primary]. Rozendaal's colour websites are the closest formal precedent ([list](https://www.newrafael.com/internet/)) [primary for titles and years; what each does is unchecked, and the description of *Abstract Browsing* is memory]. Design research supplies the positive terms: design frictions ([Cox et al.](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/1475258/1/Design%20Frictions_CHI2016LBW_v18.camera.ready.pdf)) [record], slow technology [record], and seamful design [record]. The scroller should be placed against Weiser's ideal, "The most profound technologies are those that disappear" ([reprint](https://calmtech.com/papers/computer-for-the-21st-century)) [primary], with one distinction stated: calm technology hides to protect attention and feeds hide to capture it, and the test is whether the person can bring the hidden thing back into view.

### The Legible Web: one essay links indexing to hiding

The essay's four questions (what does it look like it does, what does it actually do, what does it hide, whose interest does it serve) can be applied unchanged to the AQI, a clustering model and a feed, and its EdgeRank passage, three public factors growing to "as many as 100,000 individual weights", is the closest thing in the author's published work to the AQI argument [primary]. The single most on-point outside source is Lialina's "Turing Complete User": the invisible computer also makes the user invisible ([essay](http://contemporary-home-computing.org/turing-complete-user/)) [primary]. Her earlier line links both halves of the thesis in one sentence, "The amateur web didn't die and it has not disappeared but it is hidden" ([A Vernacular Web](https://art.teleportacia.org/observation/vernacular/)) [primary]: ranking is the mechanism of hiding. Helmond's "platformization" is the scholarly name for the shift ([UvA](https://dare.uva.nl/id/a0f08571-f169-44a6-8099-0419bf4bcd75)) [record]; Chun, Emerson and Kittler argue that interfaces conceal the machine [record; no quotes verified]; Star explains the essay's "legible even in failure" observation, since infrastructure shows itself at breakdown [record; the phrase is from memory]. Victor gives a design principle to borrow, "People understand what they can see" ([Learnable Programming](https://worrydream.com/LearnableProgramming/)) [primary]. The measurements carry the argument better than page weight does: 19% of mobile pages point to public source maps ([Web Almanac 2024](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/javascript)) [primary], and Pew found that users who saw an AI summary "clicked on a traditional search result link in 8% of all visits" against 15% without, with 1% clicking a link inside the summary ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/)) [primary; Google disputed the study and an industry study points the other way]. The Pew figure is the strongest evidence anywhere in the notes for an answer layer that is rarely opened back to its sources.

Precedents here are practices more than artworks: Low-tech Magazine's solar site, whose background shows the battery level ([site](https://solar.lowtechmagazine.com/about/the-solar-website/)) [primary]; Moll's *The Hidden Life of an Amazon User*, 1,307 requests and 8,724 pages of code for one purchase ([project](https://www.janavirgin.com/AMZ/)) [primary]; HTML Energy, *The HTML Review*, the IndieWeb and permacomputing [primary]; and the author's own site, whose backlinks after Nelson are a small working example of what the essay asks for. The counter-position is reputable. Nielsen's progressive disclosure "defers advanced or rarely used features to a secondary screen" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/)) [primary]; Voss argues frameworks let more people build ([reply](https://seldo.com/posts/the_case_for_frameworks)) [primary]; Litt suggests language models, an opaque layer, may hand authorship to many more people ([essay](https://www.geoffreylitt.com/2023/03/25/llm-end-user-programming.html)) [primary]. The reply is that Nielsen and Weiser both assume the person can call the hidden layer forward. The honest concession is that most people never viewed source, and nearly all the handmade-web essays are by people who can already code.

## The hardest objections, and where they land on felt air

Five objections have published support, and the refined thesis answers each better than the broad one does. **Compression is a service.** Simon: "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention" ([full text](https://www.gwern.net/doc/design/1971-simon.pdf)) [primary]. Answer: agreed; summary first, detail on demand, which is Shneiderman's "overview first, zoom and filter, then details on demand" ([UMD](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/items/155a868e-fb83-4115-9899-9187ea8c0498)) [record]. **Numbers build trust between strangers.** Porter argues quantification spreads where experts are distrusted, and impersonal numbers restrain arbitrary judgement ([Project MUSE](https://muse.jhu.edu/book/77331)) [record; the "technology of distance" sentence is unverified]. Answer: felt air keeps the population number on screen as a reference; it does not delete it. **The AQI works.** Smog alerts reduce attendance at outdoor facilities ([Neidell](https://jhr.uwpress.org/content/44/2/450)) [record; effect sizes not retrieved]; awareness rises with exposure and adults with asthma respond more, 24.6% against 14.0% ([Mirabelli et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7182097/)) [primary]; in Louisville the AQI itself predicted inhaler use [primary]. Answer: concede it. The index succeeds as a broadcast to a population and falls short as information for one person. The same literature gives the limit: median adherence to alert advice is 31%, and many people act on their own perception of the air instead ([D'Antoni et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5610416/)) [primary]; a Toronto analysis found alerts alone had limited health benefit ([record](https://doaj.org/article/e4853ad0e53b4448b642e56dc0feb176)) [record]. A diary that formalises bodily judgement works with observed behaviour. **Simplified risk formats help patients.** Gigerenzer and colleagues [record]. Answer: their formats work because they keep the reference class visible, which is a small case of a summary that opens. **Transparency is a naive goal.** Ananny and Crawford; Kaur; Bansal. Answer: the claim is a route back and a way to dispute, not an explanation panel that asks for trust.

The argument applies to felt air, and the ledger already lists where. The notes add sources to each row, and it is stronger to say these aloud than to wait for the question.

**Baselines hidden from the journal** is the weakest row and the sources agree. The comparison rests on moments the person cannot see or correct in the app, which fails the contestability test (Kluttz; Lyons, Velloso and Miller, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.01774) [primary, abstract]) and is the high-assistance case in which Coyle found felt agency drops. **Counts reachable only by export** meets the letter of (a) and strains its spirit: three steps and a file format is further than Nielsen's "only if a user asks", and Gouveia's finding that most use is a glance means few will go. There is a sharper point underneath. "8 of 14 flare days" is a frequency statement with its reference class attached, the format Gigerenzer recommends, and felt air hides it; that reading is this report's inference from the notes. The defence of words-not-counts is therefore an argument and not a finding: quantified feedback helps goal pursuit, a rescue-inhaler diary has no goal to pursue, so the cost side (Ancker, Etkin) dominates. Knapp's result concerned words for the probability of future harm, while felt air's words describe a frequency in the person's own past; no study was found on verbal frequency terms applied to one's own past data. **Good / OK / Not good** is, as the ledger says, the AQI's move at small scale; the difference is one tap to the value. **The three-step verdict** cannot be opened to its score, which puts it nearer Oura's bands than the argument would like. **Archetype names** invite Hacking's looping: a name people take up and live by. **Tags suggested silently** sits badly beside Bellotti and Edwards's requirement that a system represent "what they know, how they know it" [wording secondary] and beside the author's own essay on systems that fail to say what they are ([Pseudanthropy](https://pandji.co/writing/pseudanthropy)) [primary]; confirmation by the person is the defence. **The halftone dots** are the one case where hiding prevents a false reveal, and *In the Air*'s interpolation is the contrast to show. From April's redesign, the "calm" note judges the day by the EPA's Good band, everyone's number, and the 2024 breakpoint change shows that band is a policy setting.

Two critics go to the whole project, not to a row. Kenner studied asthma apps and worries that tracking moves responsibility for polluted air onto the individual breather [record, via publisher material and a review]; Liboiron argues threshold thinking itself grants permission to pollute, and warns against swapping one threshold for another [record, via reviews]. The answer is in the design, and it should be said plainly: the population threshold stays visible beside the personal one, there is no risk percentage, the language is resemblance and not prediction, and the app says it is not medical advice. Mamykina's finding that a diabetes tool moved people toward an internal locus of control [record*] means the talk should separate control over interpretation from responsibility for attacks. A personal threshold learned from a few dozen logs is also statistically fragile; the gate is the design answer and should be presented as one.

## Conclusion
